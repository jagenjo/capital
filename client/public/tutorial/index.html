The idea

You run a company in a procedural world of cities, farmland, forests, mines and ports. You buy land, build farms, mines, factories and shops, hire workers, set prices, sign contracts and compete with AI companies and other players. The game is inspired by Capitalism (1995) and is full of micromanagement: almost every number you see can be changed.

The game is turn based. You plan everything you want to do in a month, then press End turn. The server simulates the month: production, shipping, sales, wages, taxes, growth of cities. A server also plays one turn automatically every day (configurable by the admin), and can advance sooner when all players are ready.

Win by reaching the net-worth goal first, or by being the richest when the game's length ends. Three months in a row of negative net worth means bankruptcy.

Joining a game

Open the home page. Enter your name and a personal password (3+ characters), a company name, a CEO portrait and a company colour, then press Join / log in on a game. The same name and password log you back in from any browser. If a game has a lock icon, type its game password too.

To create a game, use the admin panel (default password admin). You can set the starting cash, the number of AI companies, game length, net-worth goal, turn interval and the Starter business option (a ready-made farm, factory and shop so you can watch the economy run from turn one).

The screen

  • Top bar: your company, date and turn, cash, net worth, debt, time to the next automatic turn, and how many players are ready. End turn marks you ready; Leave returns to the home screen (your company stays).
  • Tab strip: Map, Desk, My buildings, Supply chain, Market, Personnel, Finance, Analytics, Stock market, Real estate, Advertising, Competitors, News, Encyclopedia, Settings. Use the arrows at the ends when the window is narrow.
  • Map tab has four panels: the world minimap (top left), the zoom view (top right), tile or company information (bottom left) and the actions for the selected tile or building (bottom right). Drag the grip in the centre to resize the panels; double-click it to reset.
The game remembers the tile you last clicked and the map position, so you continue where you left off.

How a turn works

  1. Plan: buy land, order buildings, set prices, orders and suppliers, hire people, answer your Desk.
  2. End turn: you are marked as ready. When everybody is ready (or the daily timer fires) the month is simulated. Press again to unready.
  3. Simulation: farms and mines produce; factories turn inputs into products; goods move between buildings; shops sell to the people in their area; wages, rent, maintenance, interest and taxes are paid; cities grow or shrink; events happen.
  4. Review: a turn summary pops up, the News tab lists what happened, and each building has a report.

Construction takes time: most buildings finish after 1 month, mines, quarries and oil wells take 2, and malls take 3. A building that is still under construction shows "opens in N mo".

Reading the map

The world is a 400×240 tile map: seas, beaches, farmland, forests, hills, mountains, ore deposits, oil fields and snow peaks. Cities are groups of downtown, suburb and town tiles, ports sit on the coast, and roads and railways connect the cities.

  • Minimap: click or drag to move the zoom view. The yellow box shows what the zoom view displays.
  • Zoom view: click a tile to select it. Use the buttons for zoom in/out, centre on your buildings, and the whole world. The drop-down chooses the overlay (Terrain, population, pollution, transport, fertility…) and Show toggles labels and markers.
  • Small coloured badges are buildings: yours have your colour, rivals show theirs. A red line shows the transport route when a building is selected.

Tiles and land

Every tile is a lot you can buy and build on. Click one to see its stats on the left and what you can build on the right.

StatMeaning
FertilityHow good the soil is. Farms harvest more on fertile land.
Population herePeople living in this tile (cities only). Drives land price and customers.
Transport availability / costHow well road and rail serve the tile, and what it costs to move goods to the nearest city.
PollutionFactories pollute. Forests absorb up to 70% of it nearby.
Price to build hereLand plus construction. In cities land is priced by population: twice the people, twice the price.

Ordering a building

Press Order. The cost box under the button shows Land, Installation (construction), Taxes (4% on both) and the Total. Several companies can order the same lot in the same turn: type a Sealed bid +$ and the highest bid wins when the turn is played.

  • Farm / logging / mines / oil / quarry must sit on the right terrain (farmland, forest, ore deposit, oil field, mountain).
  • Factories can go on open land, forest and in cities. In the city a downtown factory is always 2×2 cells; suburbs and towns offer 2×2 or 3×3; open land 2×2 to 4×4.
  • Stores need people: they sell to the population within a few tiles. Supermarkets and malls reach farther.
  • R&D labs research new technology. Malls host tenants.

Cities and growth

Each tile with people has needs: bread, dairy, meat, clothing and other everyday goods. Cities grow when shops actually sell those goods to a tile; they shrink when needs go unmet. A growing suburb spreads into open land around it, and becomes downtown when none is left. Production nearby raises the chance of growth.

The Growth box in the tile panel lists what the tile needs and how much of it is being sold, so you can see why a place is not growing.

The workforce is a pool per city. Wages rise when companies compete for workers. A building belongs to its nearest city (labour, wages and customers).

Farms, mines and other raw producers

Select your building (click it on the map or in My buildings). A card with tabs appears at the lower right.

  • Product: grain, corn, cotton, vegetables, fruits, sugar or livestock for farms (depending on fertility).
  • Lines: each adds capacity, at a cost. Automation (robots) cuts labour for extra cost.
  • Sales: Sell to everyone puts your stock on the open market at your price; Sell to world market exports at world prices; you can also sell directly to your own factories and stores or by contract.
  • Farms burn some fuel to run. It is bought from the market automatically if you do not supply it.

Factories

A factory turns inputs into a product. Its recipe comes from the product (for example bread needs grain; beverages need fruit and sugar; canned food needs vegetables). Check the Market and Encyclopedia tabs for recipes.

  • Inputs: for every input choose a source: a harbour import, one of your own farms or mines, or a company that sells to everyone. Nothing is bought without an explicit source.
  • Maintenance % keeps the condition up. A low condition lowers output.
  • Staff are required per line, robot and other module. Understaffed factories produce less.
A factory sells only through its export cells (see below). Without an export cell, goods pile up unsold.

Factory layouts

The Layout tab is a small grid of cells. Click a cell to see its info or to build a module in it. Modules are:

ModuleRole
Production lineMakes one product from the products that arrows bring in. Its output limit and quality effort (50–150%) can be set.
Import cellBuys one product from a chosen source and holds a small buffer.
WarehouseStores 10× more than other cells. It holds only what arrows bring it and can throw goods away.
Export cellWhere finished goods are put on sale. Set its price here. Goods leave the factory only through export cells.
Robot, QA, Branding, R&D benchSupport modules: link them to a line with a plain connection to give automation, quality, brand or research.

Arrows

Click the bar between two neighbouring cells (or the diagonal in a corner) to cycle: arrow one way, arrow the other way, a plain link (support modules), or none. A line pulls inputs only from the cells that point at it. A line's output is split equally between the warehouses and export cells it points at. Lines can feed other lines, e.g. electronics made inside a car factory.

Cells show warning signs when something is wrong (no source, nothing to produce from, a full buffer). The tab also lists problems in text; fix them until it says "All lines are supplied".

Retail stores

Stores sell goods to the people living around them. Shelves compete with an NPC retailer and other shops on price, quality, brand and location.

Shelves

  • Price shows the difference to the market reference price. Cheaper sells more, dearer earns more per unit.
  • Order/month is how many units the store buys from its supplier. If you order less than people want, it shows "unmet" demand.
  • A shelf with no source buys nothing: pick a supplier (your factory, another company, or a harbour import).

Desk and contracts

Customers, distributors, TV shows and others send you offers: Sign, Decline, or do nothing (each shows what happens by default). Contracts guarantee sales above market price but you must deliver every month; if you fail, the other party can take you to fault. The Desk also holds the Forecast, prepared by your advisor (hire one in Personnel).

Supply chain

Nodes are production lines, farms, mines, shops and harbours. A link means goods flow along it. Hover a node to highlight its links, click it to open the building. A green note says "Every input has a source"; otherwise it points at missing links.

Market

Filter by Food, Resources, Manufacture or Technology. "Produced on the map" lists what somebody makes; "Not produced yet" shows opportunities. Click a product to open its card.

The right-hand pie shows units sold last month. The World Market tab shows prices and volumes of the export market, which drops as you sell more.

Personnel

Candidates are hired by sealed bids: type a monthly salary and press Bid. When the turn is played the highest bid for each candidate wins and gets a contract of 12–24 months. Managers raise a building's efficiency, productivity and cut expenses. Advisors (market analyst, financial advisor, operations consultant, competitive intelligence) give you insights and the forecast.

Finance

  • Balance: cash, debt, net worth and credit limit. Overdraft is allowed but charges interest.
  • Credit offers: banks, credit unions, development funds and venture capital, each with its own rate, term and requirements (some need collateral). Open an offer, choose amount and term, and sign.
  • Fiscal year-end policy: how much profit is paid as dividends or reinvested.

Analytics

Use it to find what makes money and what loses it, and to see profit after tax.

Stock market

Every company has 1,000,000 shares. You can buy or sell shares of others (with sealed bids), and issue your own shares to raise cash. Holding more than 50% lets you merge. Share prices follow net worth and recent profits.

Real estate

Used facilities are cheaper than building from scratch (typically 30–55% less) but come empty of staff and worn. Listings expire after 8 months. City incentives are one-off discounts and tax breaks for the first company of a kind in a city.

Advertising

Brand awareness decays about 8% a month, so campaigns must be sustained. Set a budget per product, or sign a contract with a newspaper, radio or television outlet to push one product for a number of months (cancelling early costs 2 months of fee).

Competitors

You can send private messages, propose contracts, or buy their shares. AI companies fill the market, supply raw goods and open shops that grow cities.

Settings and console

The console accepts commands. Type /help for the list. Currency only changes how amounts are displayed; the game always calculates in dollars.

Your first 12 months

  1. Look at the Market: pick a basic good with demand (bread, dairy, packaged meat).
  2. Click a city and see its Growth box: unmet needs tell you what to supply.
  3. Build a farm on farmland near a city (or use the starter business). Set its product and sell price.
  4. Build a factory; in Layout, make sure the chain is Import → Production → Export. Set the import source to your farm and an export price.
  5. Build a retail store downtown. Add the product to a shelf, choose your factory as the supplier, set price and order.
  6. Hire staff, check the Issues column in My buildings, then press End turn.
  7. Read last month's Report. Adjust price, order and quality effort. Repeat; reinvest the profits in a second shop or product.

Tips

  • Keep an eye on cash: construction, wages and loans can bankrupt you. Three negative net-worth months in a row end the game for you.
  • Price slightly below the reference price to win customers; raise it when stock runs low.
  • Never leave a shelf without a supplier, or a factory without an export cell.
  • Quality, brand and location matter as much as price. Decoration and advertising raise brand.
  • Forests lower pollution; put factories near them, but away from dense downtown if you want cheap land.
  • Rival AI prices are visible in the Competitors tab: undercut them when you can supply more cheaply.

FAQ

My factory produces but earns nothing. It has no export cell, or the export cell has no price. Open the Layout tab.

My store shows unmet demand. The shelf order is smaller than what customers want. Raise Order/month, or the supplier does not have enough stock.

The city is not growing. Select a tile: the Growth box shows which basic goods are not being sold there.

Can I play from another computer? Yes: use the same name and password on the home page.

Where is the encyclopedia of products and tiles? In the game's Encyclopedia tab; tiles.html shows every map tile.
